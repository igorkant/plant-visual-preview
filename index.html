  • DLE plant — 1000 m³/ddle-1000.html
    Brine at 50 ppm Li on a ten-column skid: 100 BV/h through radial columns off a single valve manifold, ~4 of the 10 on duty at any instant, 2 h sorption at 85 % recovery, with NMR analysers, dosing skids and a self-cleaning filter on the feed line — isolation valves either side, a normally closed bypass on tees, backwash to its own tank.
  • DLE pilot unit — 32 m³/hdle-pilot.html
    Ten radial-flow columns of 20 kg sorbent in two banks of five, built from a process flow diagram: in each bank four run on brine at 4 m³/h — 0.2 m³/h per kg, the rate the sorption window was set at — while the fifth is washed, desorbed and washed again on a separate service header, and the slot moves along the row. Feed passes three cartridge stages in series — 10, 1 and 0.1 µm — each a 20″ Big Blue housing with a gauge either side, isolation valves at both ends of the train and a normally closed bypass on tees that no phase opens: at change-out the pump stops and the housings drain by gravity through one collector into the waste tank. The valve station carries five headers: brine and service overhead, eluate, waste and drain below, filled by gravity. The columns and the valve station are one skid, laid out like a packaged membrane unit: two tight rows of columns on a low stainless frame with the headers running between them — supply overhead, collectors below — so every valve stays inside the frame, and each column's four-valve cross folded into the plane right behind it, with a short gooseneck onto its head and its outlet dropping down its own axis. Every channel has a four-valve cross that reverses the flow through its column, outside-in or inside-out. Waste leaves on one header and the drains on another: the collectors are vented, shallow and set below the headers they take, so the gravity lines fall into them instead of climbing. Sorption runs 1 to 11 h, set by the 3 to 40 ppm of lithium in the feed; at the rich end two columns of a bank share the service header. Nothing here is actuated — 100 manual ball valves, local gauges, a relief valve set at 1.8 barg on each pump and a switch that trips it at 1.7. The 2D P&ID view is drawn as a schematic: ISA balloons, tagged valves, stream numbers from the document's stream table and a title block for XL-PFD-001 Rev E.
  • Lead-lag DLE pilot — 2 m³/hleadlag-pilot.html
    Two lead→lag pairs of radial-flow columns, 10 kg of sorbent each, for brine at 20–40 ppm Li. The pilot column has the same diameter and the same radial bed thickness as the full-size one and only a quarter of its bed height, and every column runs at the same 0.1 m³/h per kg — so breakthrough curves taken here carry straight over to the skid and the plant. Alkali dosing, a pH probe and a cartridge filter block — three 20″ Big Blue housings at 100 / 50 / 25 µm on a stand, cartridges changed on ΔP, isolation valves either side — ahead of a manual 2+2 valve battery; regeneration tanks off the skid; grab-sample points at the feed and after every column; the full eight-phase lead-lag cycle animated.
  • Lead-lag DLE skid — 20 m³/hleadlag-skid.html
    Five lead→lag pairs on one membrane-style skid: ten 2.0 m radial columns of 40 kg in two rows either side of an actuated valve battery — 14 valves per pair, with LEAD/LAG plates that swap with the cycle — and an operator for scale. The brine arrives through a flowmeter and a flow-control valve holding the skid's 20 m³/h, after alkali dosing (NaOH / Na₂CO₃ / NaHCO₃, chosen on the pilot) and a self-cleaning filter — its housings backwashed one at a time into a manifold to the waste tank, ΔP across the filter starting the backwash, isolation valves and a normally closed bypass on tees; water, acid, eluate and waste tanks stand off the skid. Each pair runs on its own clock, staggered by 2 h, so exactly one pair is regenerating at any moment — a lane strip under the phase chips shows every pair live. The Guide button walks through the skid step by step, with the camera, markers and flow labels, in English or Russian.
  • Lead-lag DLE system — 200 m³/hleadlag-system.html
    Ten skids identical to the 20 m³/h one — 100 columns, 50+50. Three brine sources are equalised in one tank; three brine pumps, two duty and one standby, discharge through check valves into one common header that feeds two pretreatment trains of 100 m³/h (alkali dosing, pH, a self-cleaning filter with a backwash manifold, ΔP, isolation valves and a normally closed bypass on tees); each train can be isolated by hand valves, so a pump trip stops nothing and a train under repair takes out no row. Each train feeds a brine header on the pipe rack along its row of five skids; every skid draws its own 20 m³/h through a branch with an isolation valve, a flowmeter and a flow-control valve, so the skids run in parallel, never in series. Regeneration water and acid are distributed, and raffinate, eluate and waste collected, through headers on the same portal rack. A normally closed cross-tie on tees joins the two brine headers, so one train can carry part of the other row while its own train is down. Skids and pairs cycle staggered in time — at any moment 10 of the 50 pairs are regenerating — and a Guide walks through the plant in English or Russian.
  • Floating sorption skidpontoon.html
    Pilot on a modular floating platform on HDPE floats, battery-powered with two remote-controlled thrusters — three filtration stages (10 / 1 / 0.1 µm, cleaned or changed on ΔP) with isolation valves and a normally closed bypass on tees, the flush going overboard, valve manifold feeding four radial columns on its own tap lines. Sorption is the only duty afloat: Dead Sea brine is the sole medium on deck, and no reagent cube ever comes aboard. Each column holds 20 kg (22 L) of sorbent at 9 m³/h (≈410 BV/h), on a flat rigid FRP frame anchored to the deck: the outlet leaves the bottom head on the axis and runs in the open to the lower header, so the path off each column can be followed by eye. The feed drops on a flexible hose ending in a Banjo Dry-Mate 2″ dry-disconnect coupling, its latches without steel. Each column carries two lifting lugs under its top flange — plates with an open hole for a shackle pin — and parts at a quick coupling top and bottom, so a loaded one is craned ashore on the pier’s 3 m knuckle boom and a regenerated one takes its place — desorption happens ashore. The crane picks on a spreader beam with a hook at each end, set to that lug pitch, so both padeyes take the load and the vessel comes up plumb. The pier itself runs out from the shore: a loading head on piles where the pontoon lies alongside, and an approach trestle back to the bank the columns leave over. The outlet under every column is piped in the open: down through the frame, out past its beams and into the lower header, which puts the depleted brine over the side. Dimensions, payload and freeboard of the platform are set by its supplier’s engineering.
  • Floating plant — 200 columnspontoon-200.html
    The Dead Sea scheme at full scale on a 10 × 40 m pontoon: 200 radial columns of 50 kg (Ø0.4 × 2 m, 0.2 m between shells, 29 mm radial bed) on five skids of 40, all in parallel — no lead-lag. Each column has its own six actuated valves (brine, water and HCl onto its head; raffinate, eluate and waste off its bottom); the two columns of a slot run in antiphase, one sorbing for about an hour at 450 BV/h while the other is washed, desorbed and rinsed, so 100 columns sorb at any moment and the plant takes ≈2045 m³/h. Brine rises through a DN600 moon-pool intake to two duty pumps and one standby, is pH-dosed and filtered (10 / 1 / 0.1 µm, cleaned or changed on ΔP, normally closed bypass), and is shared out by headers in the aisle between the skid groups; the raffinate drops overboard. The HCl, eluate and waste tanks stand at the moored end by the pier; fresh water comes from shore by pipe and power by cable. Tank sizes and regeneration rates are estimates.
  • Floating plant — 200 columns, Dead Sea modespontoon-200-deadsea.html
    The same 200-column pontoon on the steps of the Dead Sea skid: sorption 1.5 h → water wash 0.2 h → desorption with 0.8 M H₂SO₄ 0.5 h in five fractions → NaCl wash pH 10 0.2 h, a 2.4 h cycle. The water wash goes to the spent brine; Fr-2/3 go to the eluate tank, Fr-1, Fr-4/5 and the NaCl wash to the sulfate tank. Each column has seven actuated valves: brine, water, H₂SO₄ and NaCl onto its head, raffinate, eluate and sulfate off its bottom. A seventh header carries the NaCl wash from its own tank and pump (T-203, P-202). Five of every eight columns sorb (125 of 200) at 450 BV/h, so the plant takes ≈2557 m³/h through a DN700 intake. Tank sizes and regeneration rates are estimates.
