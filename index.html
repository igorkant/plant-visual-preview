  • DLE pilot unit — 2 m³/hdle-pilot.html
    Five radial-flow columns of 2.5 kg sorbent, built to a process flow diagram: four run on brine at 0.5 m³/h each while the fifth is washed, desorbed and washed again on a separate service header, and the slot moves along the row. Feed passes three cartridge stages in series — 10, 1 and 0.1 µm — each its own housing with a gauge either side and its own drain, bypassed as a train for change-out. The valve station carries five headers: brine and service overhead, eluate, waste and drain below, filled by gravity. The columns stand on one frame, five seats in its bed, each discharging down its own axis into the frame instead of out of its side. Every channel has a four-valve cross that reverses the flow through its column, outside-in or inside-out. Waste leaves on one header and the drains on another: the collectors are vented, shallow and set below the headers they take, so the gravity lines fall into them instead of climbing. Sorption runs 1 to 11 h, set by the 3 to 40 ppm of lithium in the feed; at the rich end two columns share the service header. Nothing here is actuated — 55 manual ball valves, local gauges, a relief valve set at 1.8 barg on each pump and a switch that trips it at 1.7. The 2D P&ID view is drawn as a schematic: ISA balloons, tagged valves, stream numbers from the document's stream table and a title block for XL-PFD-001 Rev E.
  • Floating sorption skidpontoon.html
    Pilot on a moored pontoon, 5.8 × 5.8 m of deck — moon-pool seawater intake, self-cleaning strainers on a bypass, valve manifold feeding four radial columns on its own tap lines. Sorption is the only duty afloat: seawater is the sole medium on deck, and no reagent cube ever comes aboard. Each column holds 20 kg (22 L) of sorbent at 9 m³/h, on a flat rigid frame anchored to the deck: the outlet leaves the bottom head straight into the collection header buried in that frame, so nothing pipes to the underside of a vessel. The feed drops on a flexible hose ending in a GF Quick Connect Valve 700 coupling. A 3 m knuckle-boom loader on the pier swaps loaded beds for regenerated ones — desorption happens ashore. The deck is sized to the skid rather than the other way round: 34 m², with the columns, the manifold over the moon pool, the pump and the strainers packed into it and a walkway left round the rail.
